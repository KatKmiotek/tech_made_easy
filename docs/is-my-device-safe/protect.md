# Protect Your Device & Your Stuff

Imagine your phone disappeared tomorrow.

Maybe you left it on a bus, dropped it somewhere, or it simply stopped working.

Ask yourself:

> **Could someone get into it?**

And...

> **Would you lose anything important?**

These are two different problems — and we can protect ourselves against both.

---

## Lock your device

Your phone, tablet and computer should automatically lock when you're not using them.

You can usually protect them with:

- A PIN
- A password
- Fingerprint recognition
- Face recognition

### Don't make your PIN too easy to guess

Avoid things like:

- `1234`
- `0000`
- Your year of birth
- Simple patterns

A longer PIN is harder for someone else to guess.

---

## Fingerprint and Face ID

Using your fingerprint or face can make good security **easier**, not harder.

Instead of typing your PIN every time, you can unlock your device quickly while still keeping it protected.

Your PIN or password remains important as a backup.

:::tip
If you currently don't use a screen lock because entering a password is annoying, fingerprint or face recognition might be a much easier option.
:::

---

## Make sure it locks automatically

A PIN isn't much help if your phone stays unlocked for hours after you put it down.

Most devices can automatically lock after a short period of inactivity.

Look in settings for:

**Screen lock**, **Auto-Lock**, **Lock screen** or **Screen timeout**.

---

# What if my phone is lost or stolen?

Modern phones have tools that can help you find them.


### Apple — iPhone, iPad and Mac

**Find My**

Depending on your device and settings, Find My can help you locate a missing Apple device, play a sound, mark it as lost or remotely erase it.

### Android

**Find Hub**

Depending on your device and settings, these services can help you:

- See approximately where your device is
- Make it play a sound
- Mark it as lost
- Lock it remotely
- Erase it remotely if necessary

:::note
These features need to be set up before you lose your device.

Later we'll check whether they're enabled.
:::

# Would you lose all your photos?

Now imagine your phone hasn't been stolen.

It simply stops working.

What would disappear with it?

- Photos and videos?
- Contacts?
- Documents?
- Notes?
- Important messages?

This is where **backups** become important.

---

## What is a backup?

A backup is simply:

> **Another copy of something important.**

Your device might automatically keep copies of your information online.

You may already be using:

- **iCloud** on Apple devices
- **Google** services on Android
- **OneDrive** on Windows

You might already have a backup without realizing it.

---

## The cloud isn't the same as a backup

This is an important distinction.

Some services **synchronize** your files between devices.

That means deleting something on one device may also delete it from the online copy.

A proper backup gives you a way to **recover information when something goes wrong**.

So don't just ask:

> "Are my photos in the cloud?"

Also ask:

> **"If I accidentally delete something, can I get it back?"**

---

## Check your photos

Photos are often the thing people are most upset about losing.

On your phone, check whether your photos are being backed up or synchronized using something like:

- iCloud Photos
- Google Photos
- OneDrive

Also check whether you have enough storage space.

A backup that stopped six months ago because the account ran out of space isn't much help!

---

# What about my computer?

Think about what is actually stored on your computer.

Would you lose anything important if it stopped working tomorrow?

For important files, consider keeping another copy:

- In OneDrive, iCloud or Google Drive
- On an external drive
- On another trusted device

For especially important things, having **more than one copy** is sensible.

---

# The simple version

To protect the **device**:

### Lock it with a PIN, password, fingerprint or face recognition.

### Let it lock automatically.

### Enable the device-finding feature.

To protect your **stuff**:

### Keep another copy of important files and photos.

### Check that your backups are actually working.
