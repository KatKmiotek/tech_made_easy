# Apps, Downloads & Permissions

We install apps and programs all the time.

But before installing something, it is worth asking:

> **Where did this come from, and do I trust it?**

---

## Where should I get apps from?

On phones and tablets, the safest starting point is the official app store:

- **iPhone / iPad** — Apple App Store
- **Android** — Google Play Store
- **Windows** — Microsoft Store, or the software company's official website

Official stores check apps before allowing them onto their platforms.

This doesn't make every app perfect, but it reduces the risk of accidentally installing malicious software.

---

## Be careful when searching for downloads

Imagine you want to install **Zoom**.

You search Google for:

> download Zoom

The first result you see isn't always necessarily the official website.

Look at:

- The name of the website
- The web address
- Whether it really belongs to the company
- Whether the result says **Sponsored**

:::tip
If you know the company that makes the software, try to download it directly from their official website or app store.
:::

---

## Do I really need this app?

Every app you install is another piece of software on your device.

It's a good idea occasionally to look through your apps and remove ones you:

- Don't use anymore
- Don't remember installing
- No longer need

If you don't recognize something on a Windows computer, **don't immediately delete it**.

Some unfamiliar programs are needed by Windows or by your computer's hardware.

---

# What are app permissions?

When you install or use an app, it might ask:

> **Allow this app to access your location?**

or:

> **Allow access to your camera and microphone?**

This isn't automatically something bad.

The important question is:

### Does it make sense?

---

## Some permissions are completely normal

A video calling app needs access to your:

**Camera and microphone**

A maps app needs your:

**Location**

A photo editing app may need access to your:

**Photos**

These permissions make sense for what the app does.

---

## But what if it doesn't make sense?

Imagine you install a simple **torch app** and it asks for access to:

- Your contacts
- Your microphone
- Your precise location

Ask yourself:

> **Why would a torch need that?**

If you can't see a good reason, you don't have to press **Allow**.

You can choose **Don't Allow** and change the permission later if the app actually needs it.

---

## "Allow while using the app"

You may also see choices such as:

- **Allow once**
- **Allow while using the app**
- **Always allow**
- **Don't allow**

You don't always need to give an app permanent access.

For example, a maps app may need your location while you're using it, but that doesn't necessarily mean it needs to know your location all the time.

---

## You can change your mind

Giving permission once doesn't mean it has to stay that way forever.

### iPhone / iPad

Go to:

**Settings → Privacy & Security**

Here you can see which apps have access to things such as:

- Location
- Camera
- Microphone
- Photos
- Contacts

### Android

The exact menu varies between phones, but look for:

**Settings → Privacy → Permission manager**

or search Settings for **"permissions"**.

### Windows

Go to:

**Settings → Privacy & security → App permissions**

---

## What about links that download something automatically?

Be cautious if a website unexpectedly tells you to:

> **Download this to continue**

or:

> **Install this security software immediately**

Don't install it just because a website tells you to.

Close the message and think about whether you actually asked to download something.

---

# A simple rule

Before installing an app or allowing access, ask:

### 1. Where did this come from?

### 2. Do I trust it?

### 3. Does what it's asking for make sense?

If you're unsure, **it's OK to say no**.

You can usually change your mind later.
