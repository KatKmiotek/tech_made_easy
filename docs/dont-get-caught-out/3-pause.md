# The PAUSE Test

A simple habit for checking suspicious messages.

# P — Pressure

Ask:

> **Are they trying to make me act quickly?**

Warning phrases:

- immediately
- final warning
- within 24 hours
- act now
- your account will close
- your money is at risk

> **Urgency is the scammer's friend. Time is yours.**

# A — Ask

Ask:

> **What do they want from me?**

Are they asking for:

- money?
- card details?
- password?
- one-time security code?
- personal information?
- a click?
- a download?
- remote access?
- moving the conversation somewhere else?

# U — Unexpected

Ask:

> **Was I expecting this?**

Examples:

- Am I expecting a parcel?
- Did I enter this competition?
- Did I request this refund?
- Did I contact this company?
- Does this person normally speak to me this way?

Unexpected does not automatically mean scam.

It means:

> **Stop and check.**

# S — Source

Ask:

> **Who is really contacting me?**

Do not rely only on:

- company logo
- sender display name
- profile picture
- familiar-looking phone number
- caller ID

Scammers can impersonate trusted organisations and people.

# E — Exit & Verify

The most important step.

> **Leave the message and verify another way.**

For example:

- open the bank app yourself
- type the website address yourself
- use the phone number on the back of your card
- use a paper bill or statement
- call the family member on their normal saved number
- ask somebody you trust

---

# Golden Rule

> **Do not use the suspicious message to prove that the suspicious message is genuine.**

Use contact details you found independently.
