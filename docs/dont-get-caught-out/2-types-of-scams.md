# Scams Come in Many Disguises

Scammers use familiar organizations, everyday problems and ordinary emotions.

## Delivery Scams

Common names used:

- Royal Mail
- Evri
- DPD
- other courier companies

Typical message:

> "We couldn't deliver your parcel. Pay a small redelivery fee."

What might they really want?

- card details
- address
- phone number
- bank information

**Have you noticed that we get lots of them around Xmas? Why?**

## Bank and Payment Scams

Again, often impersonation of well know brands: Amazon, PayPal - we all use them.  
Typical messages:

- suspicious transaction
- account frozen
- card compromised
- money must be moved to a "safe account"

The pressure is often:

> **"Your money is in danger. Act now."**


## HMRC and Government Scams

Examples:

- tax refund
- unpaid tax
- benefit problem
- council payment
- fine

These scams use:

- authority
- fear
- unexpected money

## Family or Friend Impersonation

Examples:

- "Hi Mum"
- "I've changed my phone number"
- urgent bill
- stranded relative
- social media account taken over

These scams use:

> **Trust + emotion + urgency**

## Tech Support Scams

The caller may claim to be from:

- Microsoft
- your broadband provider
- antivirus support
- another technology company

They may say:

> "Your computer has a serious problem."

They may ask to:

- install software
- give them remote access
- log in to online banking
- make a payment

## Facebook Marketplace and Shopping Scams

Warning signs:

- price is unusually low
- buyer sends a strange courier/payment link
- seller asks to leave the normal platform
- payment must be made quickly
- fake customer-support account contacts you

## Investment Scams

Examples:

- cryptocurrency
- celebrity-endorsed investments
- "guaranteed" returns
- fake trading platforms

The scam may begin with:

- an advert
- a social media message
- a new friendship
- repeated contact over time

## Romance Scams

A relationship or emotional connection is built before money is requested.

Common reasons given:

- emergency
- medical cost
- travel
- family problem
- investment opportunity

**have you watched The Tinder Swindler?**

## QR Code Scams

A QR code is simply another way of opening a website.

> **A QR code is not automatically trustworthy.**

A fake QR code can lead to the same type of fake page as a scam link.

## Fake Social Media Adverts

Scam adverts can impersonate:

- famous shops
- local businesses
- celebrities
- investment companies
- competitions

> **Being advertised on Facebook, Instagram or Google does not prove something is genuine.**
