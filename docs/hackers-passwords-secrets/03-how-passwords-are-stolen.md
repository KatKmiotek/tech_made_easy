# How Hackers Get Passwords

When we imagine a hacker, we might picture someone sitting at a computer trying passwords one by one.

In reality, it is often much more automated.

## 1. Guessing common passwords

Attackers know that many people use predictable passwords such as:

- `password123`
- `qwerty`
- `letmein`
- names and birthdays
- football teams
- pet names
- words with `123` or `!` added at the end

Computers can try huge lists of common passwords very quickly.

---

## 2. Dictionary attacks

A dictionary attack does not just try random letters.

It tries:

- common words
- names
- common phrases
- popular password patterns
- combinations of words and numbers

So a password that looks complicated to us may still be predictable to a computer.

For example:

`Summer2026!`

looks better than `password123`, but it still follows a very common pattern.

---

## 3. Credential stuffing

This is one of the most important attacks to understand.

Imagine a website is hacked and usernames and passwords are stolen.

Criminals may then automatically try those same details on:

- email accounts
- Facebook
- online shops
- streaming services
- other websites

This is called **credential stuffing**.

### This is why reusing passwords is risky.

If one password is exposed, several accounts could be at risk.

---

## 4. Phishing

Sometimes hackers do not need to crack a password at all.

They simply convince someone to type it into a fake login page.

For example:

> “Your account has been locked. Click here to sign in.”

The page may look almost identical to the real website.

But the password is being sent to the attacker instead.

---

## 5. Passwords can also be stolen

Passwords can be taken through:

- malware
- unsafe downloads
- fake browser extensions
- compromised websites
- data breaches
- phishing pages

So even a strong password can sometimes be stolen.

That is why password security is not only about creating a difficult password.

---

## The big takeaway

### Hackers do not usually sit there guessing your password manually.

They use:

- automation
- leaked data
- common password lists
- fake login pages
- stolen account information

The best defence is to make your accounts harder to reuse and harder to take over.

---

## Quick question

Which of these sounds most dangerous to you?

- Someone guessing your password
- A company you use getting hacked
- Reusing the same password
- Clicking a fake login page

There is no single right answer — they can all lead to account access.
