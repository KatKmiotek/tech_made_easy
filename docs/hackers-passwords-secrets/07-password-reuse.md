# Why Reusing Passwords Is Risky

Using the same password in more than one place can feel convenient.

But it can turn one small problem into a much bigger one.

---

## Imagine this

You create an account on a small gardening website.

You use:

`GardenBlue2026!`

You also use the same password for:

- your email
- Facebook
- Amazon

A few months later, the gardening website is hacked.

Your email address and password are stolen.

---

## What happens next?

The attacker does not need to guess anything.

They already have:

- your email address
- your password

They can now automatically try the same details on other websites.

This is called:

## Credential stuffing

<div className="alert alert--warning" role="alert">

**One leaked password can become a key to several accounts.**

That is the real danger of password reuse.

</div>

---

## Why email is especially important

If the same password also works for your email account, the attacker may be able to:

- read your messages
- reset passwords for other accounts
- see which services you use
- take over more accounts

This is why your email password should be:

**unique**

and ideally protected with:

**two-step verification**

---

## But what if the breached website is unimportant?

That is the trap.

You may not care if someone gets into an old forum, shop or hobby website.

But if the password is reused elsewhere, that old account can still be dangerous.

<div className="alert alert--info" role="alert">

**The weakest website can expose the password you use on your strongest account.**

</div>

---

## A simple rule

You do not need every account to be treated exactly the same.

But important accounts should always have their own unique password.

Especially:

- email
- banking
- social media
- shopping accounts with saved payment details

---

## Quick question

Which is safer?

### A

One very strong password used everywhere

### B

Different strong passwords for different accounts

The safer option is:

## B

Because one breach does not automatically expose everything else.

---

## What if remembering them all is difficult?

That is exactly what password managers are for.

They can create and store unique passwords for you.

<div className="alert alert--success" role="alert">

**Best habit to remember:**

Never reuse the password for your email account anywhere else.

</div>
