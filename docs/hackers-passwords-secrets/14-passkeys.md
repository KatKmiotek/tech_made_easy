# Are Passwords Going Away?

You may already have seen websites or apps offering something called a:

## Passkey

Passkeys are designed to reduce our reliance on traditional passwords.

Instead of typing a password, you may confirm it is really you using:

- Face ID
- fingerprint
- your phone or computer PIN
- another trusted device

---

## How is this different from a password?

With a traditional password:

- you create a secret
- you type it into websites
- it can potentially be guessed, stolen or reused

With a passkey:

- there is no normal password for you to remember
- your device proves who you are
- you usually confirm using Face ID, fingerprint or your device PIN

<div className="alert alert--success" role="alert">

**One big advantage:**

There is no password for you to accidentally type into a fake login page.

</div>

---

## You may already be using them

You might see messages such as:

> Sign in with Face ID

or:

> Use your passkey

or:

> Sign in with your device

You may already be using passkeys without realizing that is what they are called.

---

## Why can passkeys be safer?

Passkeys can help protect against:

- phishing
- password guessing
- password reuse
- stolen password databases

The secret part of the passkey stays protected on your device.

---

## What if somebody creates a fake website?

With a password, a fake page can try to trick you into typing your password.

A passkey is different.

It is connected to the real website it was created for.

<div className="alert alert--info" role="alert">

This makes passkeys particularly useful against **fake login pages**.

</div>

---

## What if I lose my phone?

Passkeys can often be connected to your Apple, Google or Microsoft account and synchronized between trusted devices.

Your exact recovery options depend on the service you use.

That means your:

- device security
- account recovery details
- screen lock

are still very important.

---

## Does Face ID get sent to the website?

Normally, no.

Your fingerprint or face is used by your own device to confirm that it is you.

The website does not receive a copy of your fingerprint or face scan.

<div className="alert alert--info" role="alert">

Your device is effectively saying:

**"Yes, the owner has approved this login."**

</div>

---

## Will passwords disappear completely?

Probably not immediately.

For now, we are likely to see a mixture of:

- passwords
- two-step verification
- passkeys
- Face ID and fingerprints

Different websites are moving at different speeds.

---

## Should I use a passkey if I am offered one?

For a reputable service, using a passkey can be a good option.

But you should still:

- protect your phone or computer
- use a strong screen lock
- keep recovery details up to date
- understand how you would recover your account

---

## Quick question

Which login is harder to steal through a fake website?

### A

### B

The answer is:

## B
