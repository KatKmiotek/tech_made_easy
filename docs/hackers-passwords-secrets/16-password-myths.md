# Password Myths

There is a lot of password advice online.

Some of it is useful.

Some of it is outdated.

And some of it is simply wrong.

Let's test a few.

---

## Myth 1

### "Adding a `!` makes my password strong."

## ❌ Myth

A symbol can help, but only if the rest of the password is not predictable.

For example:

`Summer2026!`

still follows a very common pattern.

<div className="alert alert--success" role="alert">

**Better idea:**

Think about:

**length + unpredictability + uniqueness**

not just symbols.

</div>

---

## Myth 2

### "I should change my password every month."

## ❌ Usually a myth

Changing a good password constantly is not always helpful.

It can encourage patterns such as:

`Summer2026!`

then:

`Autumn2026!`

then:

`Winter2026!`

That is still predictable.

Change a password when:

- you think someone knows it
- the account has been compromised
- it has appeared in a data breach
- you reused it somewhere that was breached

---

## Myth 3

### "Nobody would bother hacking me."

## ❌ Myth

Most attacks are not aimed personally at one individual.

Criminals can use automated tools to try:

- thousands of email addresses
- leaked passwords
- common password combinations
- large lists of accounts

<div className="alert alert--info" role="alert">

You do not need to be rich or famous to be useful to a criminal.

Your email, Facebook account or contact list may still have value.

</div>

---

## Myth 4

### "A very complicated password is always better than a long one."

## ❌ Not necessarily

Compare:

`P@ss1!`

with:

`River-Cactus-Mirror-Lantern`

The first one looks complicated.

But it is short and predictable.

The second is much longer and easier to remember.

---

## Myth 5

### "If I use two-step verification, I cannot be hacked."

## ❌ Myth

Two-step verification gives you much better protection.

But nothing makes an account completely impossible to compromise.

Attackers may still try:

- phishing
- stealing active sessions
- malware
- tricking you into approving a login
- account recovery attacks

<div className="alert alert--success" role="alert">

**MFA is not magic.**

But it can make stealing your account much harder.

</div>

---

## Myth 6

### "Password managers are dangerous because all my passwords are in one place."

## ❌ Misleading

A password manager is an important account to protect.

But it allows you to use a completely different strong password for every service.

Without one, many people end up reusing passwords.

<div className="alert alert--info" role="alert">

For most people, a well-protected password manager is safer than using the same memorable password everywhere.

</div>

---

## Myth 7

### "Writing a password down is always unsafe."

## ❌ Not always

It depends where you keep it.

A notebook stored safely at home may be much better than:

- reusing the same password everywhere
- keeping passwords on a note attached to the computer
- carrying an obvious password list in your wallet

Security needs to be practical.

---

## Myth 8

### "My password is safe because nobody knows my dog's name."

## ❌ Maybe not

Think about what you share online.

Could somebody find:

- your pet's name
- birthday
- family names
- favorite football team
- places you visit
- anniversary dates

Personal information can make passwords easier to guess.

---

## Myth 9

### "A strong password means I am safe even if a website gets hacked."

## ❌ Not completely

A good website should protect stored passwords.

But if credentials are exposed, or a password is reused elsewhere, you may still have a problem.

This is why unique passwords matter.

---

## Myth 10

### "If I receive a verification code, I should give it to the company if they ask."

## ❌ Definitely not

If someone unexpectedly phones or messages you asking for a security code:

**Do not give it to them.**

That code may be the final thing stopping them from getting into your account.

<div className="alert alert--warning" role="alert">

If you did not start the login, do not approve it.

</div>

---

### "My email account deserves extra protection."

✅ **Fact**

### "An unexpected login request can be a warning sign."

✅ **Fact**

### "Face ID means the website receives a copy of my face."

❌ **Myth**

Use unique passwords.
