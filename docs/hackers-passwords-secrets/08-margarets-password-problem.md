# Margaret's Password Problem

Meet Margaret.

Margaret uses the internet every day.

She has:

- an email account
- Facebook
- Amazon
- a gardening website account

To make things easier, she uses the same password for all four:

`GardenBlue2026!`

---

## One day...

The gardening website is hacked.

Criminals steal a list containing email addresses and passwords.

Margaret does not know this has happened yet.

<div className="alert alert--info" role="alert">

**Question for the room**

Should Margaret be worried?

The gardening website itself does not contain anything important.

</div>

---

## What could happen next?

The criminals already know Margaret's:

- email address
- password

They automatically try those same details on other websites.

First they try her email.

It works.

---

## Now the problem is much bigger

Once inside Margaret's email, they may be able to:

- read her messages
- see which companies she uses
- find password reset emails
- reset passwords for other accounts

They may not even need to know the passwords for those other accounts.

---

## Then they try Facebook

The same password works there too.

Now they can message Margaret's friends.

For example:

> Hi, sorry to ask. I'm having trouble with my bank today. Could you lend me £50? I'll send it back tomorrow.

<div className="alert alert--warning" role="alert">

Margaret's friends might trust the message because it really is coming from **Margaret's account**.

</div>

---

## What should Margaret do first?

### A

Change her gardening website password

### B

Change her Facebook password

### C

Secure her email account

### D

Delete all her accounts

---

## The best place to start

### Secure the email account

Margaret should:

1. Change her email password to a completely new, unique password
2. Sign out of other devices or sessions if the service allows it
3. Check that her recovery email and phone number have not been changed
4. Turn on two-step verification
5. Check recent account activity

Then she should secure the other affected accounts.

---

## What about her other passwords?

Because Margaret reused the same password, she should assume that every account using:

`GardenBlue2026!`

could be at risk.

She should replace it everywhere.

<div className="alert alert--success" role="alert">

**This is why unique passwords are so useful.**

If Margaret had used a different password for the gardening website, the breach could have stayed a small problem.

</div>

---

## One more twist

Imagine Margaret already had two-step verification turned on for her email.

The criminals try her stolen password...

but the email provider asks for another check.

That extra step may stop them getting in.

---

## What could have protected Margaret?

- A unique email password
- Different passwords for different accounts
- A password manager
- Two-step verification
- Paying attention to unexpected login alerts

---

## The lesson

<div className="alert alert--success" role="alert">

A small website being hacked does not have to become a big problem.

**Unique passwords stop one breach spreading to other accounts.**

</div>
