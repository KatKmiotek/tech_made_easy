# What Makes a Password Strong?

After looking at a few examples, there are three things that matter most.

<div className="alert alert--success" role="alert">

**The three rules**

**1. Make it long**

**2. Make it unpredictable**

**3. Make it unique**

</div>

---

## 1. Make it long

Longer passwords are generally harder to crack.

A short password can contain capital letters, numbers and symbols and still be easier to crack than a longer password.

For example:

`P@ss1!`

It looks complicated, but it is short and predictable.

Compare it with:

`Cloud-Lantern-Coffee-River`

This is much longer and easier to remember.

---

## 2. Make it unpredictable

Avoid using things someone could easily connect to you:

- your name
- your partner's name
- your pet's name
- your birthday
- your street
- your favorite football team
- the current year

<div className="alert alert--warning" role="alert">

Information that feels private may already be visible online.

Social media can reveal more about us than we realize.

</div>

---

## 3. Make it unique

Try not to use the same password for different important accounts.

Especially:

- email
- Facebook
- shopping
- banking

If one website is breached, criminals may try the same password elsewhere.

<div className="alert alert--info" role="alert">

You do not need to remember dozens of completely different passwords.

This is where a **password manager** can help.

</div>

---

## What about special characters?

You may have seen password rules asking for:

- one capital letter
- one number
- one symbol

These can help, but they are not the whole story.

For example:

`Summer2026!`

It follows all of those rules.

But it is still quite predictable.

<div className="alert alert--success" role="alert">

**Long + unpredictable + unique**

is more important than simply adding `!` to the end of a password.

</div>

---

## The "three random words" idea

A simple way to create a memorable password is to use several unrelated words.

For example:

`Window-Piano-Lemon-Forest`

The important part is that the words should not naturally belong together.

Another example:

`Cactus-Mirror-Train-River`

Less useful would be something predictable such as:

`I-Love-My-Dog`

That is a normal phrase and could also be connected to information about you.

<div className="alert alert--warning" role="alert">

**Do not use any of the example passwords from this presentation as your real password.**

</div>

---

## Should I change my password every few months?

Not necessarily.

You should change it if:

- you think someone knows it
- an account has been compromised
- the password appeared in a data breach
- you reused it on a website that was breached

Changing passwords too often can sometimes encourage predictable patterns.

For example:

`Summer2026!`

becomes:

`Winter2026!`

and later:

`Spring2027!`

<div className="alert alert--warning" role="alert">

Changing one small part of the same password is **not** the same as creating a completely new password.

</div>

---

## Quick challenge

Which password would you rather use?

### A

`Password2026!`

### B

`Tulip-Camera-Mountain-Spoon`

Why?

Password B is longer and much less predictable.

<div className="alert alert--success" role="alert">

**Remember: Long • Unpredictable • Unique**

</div>
