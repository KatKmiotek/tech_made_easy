# Someone Got Into My Account

Imagine you notice something strange:

- a password has changed
- you see a login you do not recognize
- messages were sent from your account
- your friends say they received strange messages from you

What should you do?

## Act quickly — but do not panic

---

## 1. Change the password

Create a completely new password.

Do not just change:

`Garden2026!`

to:

`Garden2027!`

Use something:

- unique
- long
- unpredictable

---

## 2. Check where else you used that password

If the same password was used on other accounts, change those too.

Start with:

- your email
- banking
- social media
- shopping accounts

<div className="alert alert--warning" role="alert">

**If you reused the password, assume other accounts could also be at risk.**

</div>

---

## 3. Secure your email first

If several accounts may be affected, your main email account should be a priority.

Check:

- the password
- recovery email
- recovery phone number
- signed-in devices
- two-step verification

---

## 4. Sign out unfamiliar devices

Look for:

- unknown phones
- old computers
- unfamiliar locations
- devices you no longer use

If the service offers it, use:

**Sign out of all devices**

or:

**Log out of other sessions**

---

## 5. Check recovery details

An attacker may try to keep access by changing:

- your recovery email
- your recovery phone number
- security questions
- authentication methods

Make sure everything still belongs to you.

---

## 6. Turn on two-step verification

If it is not already enabled, turn it on.

This adds another layer of protection.

<div className="alert alert--success" role="alert">

A new password plus MFA is much stronger than changing the password alone.

</div>

---

## 7. Check what happened inside the account

Look for:

- messages you did not send
- purchases you did not make
- password reset emails
- forwarding rules
- changes to account settings

---

## 8. Warn people if needed

If someone used your account to contact friends or family:

Tell them.

For example:

> "My account was accessed by someone else. Please ignore any strange messages you received from me."

This can stop the scam spreading.

---

## 9. If money is involved

If you notice:

- suspicious payments
- bank transfers
- card activity

contact your bank using a trusted number or the official banking app.

Do not rely on contact details inside a suspicious email or text message.

---

## What if you cannot log in?

Use the official account recovery process.

Go directly to the real website or app.

Look for:

**Forgot password**

or:

**Recover account**

Avoid recovery links sent in unexpected messages.

---

## Quick priority check

If these three things happened at the same time:

- Facebook account accessed
- shopping account accessed
- email account accessed

Which should you secure first?

## Your email account

Because it may be used to reset passwords for other services.

---

<div className="alert alert--success" role="alert">

**Remember the order:**

1. Change the password
2. Secure your email
3. Sign out other sessions
4. Check recovery details
5. Turn on MFA
6. Check for damage
7. Warn others if needed

</div>
