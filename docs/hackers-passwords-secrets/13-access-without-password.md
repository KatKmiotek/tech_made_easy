# Can Someone Get In Without Your Password?

Sometimes, yes.

That sounds strange, but it can happen because websites do not ask for your password every time you click something.

Once you log in, the website usually remembers that you are already signed in.

---

## Think of it like a concert

You arrive at a concert.

At the entrance, you show your ticket.

Then you are given a wristband.

After that, staff do not keep asking to see your ticket.

They just look at the wristband.

Your online account can work in a similar way.

---

## The password is like the ticket

You use your password to prove who you are.

Once the website accepts it, it gives your browser something that says:

> This person is already signed in.

This is often called a:

## Session

or a:

## Session cookie

---

## Why does this matter?

If an attacker manages to steal that session, they may be able to access the account without typing the password again.

They are effectively using your digital wristband.

<div className="alert alert--info" role="alert">

**This is why changing your password does not always end every active login immediately.**

Sometimes you also need to sign out other devices or sessions.

</div>

---

## How could a session be stolen?

This can happen through things such as:

- malware
- unsafe downloads
- malicious browser extensions
- compromised devices
- certain attacks against websites or browsers

You do not need to remember the technical details.

The important point is:

## Passwords are only one part of account security.

---

## What protects you?

A few simple habits help:

- keep your phone and computer updated
- keep your browser updated
- avoid unknown browser extensions
- be careful with downloads
- use a screen lock
- remove devices you no longer use
- review active sessions if an account looks suspicious

---

## What does "sign out everywhere" do?

Many services let you see devices where your account is signed in.

You may see things such as:

- your phone
- your laptop
- an old tablet
- an unfamiliar device

Some services also offer:

**Sign out of all devices**

or:

**Log out of other sessions**

<div className="alert alert--success" role="alert">

If you think somebody accessed your account, changing the password is important.

But also check:

**Who is still signed in?**

</div>

---

## What about public computers?

If you sign into email or another important account on a computer that is not yours:

- do not choose "remember me"
- sign out when finished
- avoid saving the password
- avoid using important accounts on devices you do not trust

---

## Does two-step verification still help?

Yes.

Two-step verification can help stop someone logging in with a stolen password.

But if someone steals an already active session, they may not be asked for the second step again.

That is why:

**updates + device security + passwords + MFA**

all matter together.

---

## Quick question

Imagine you discover an unfamiliar device signed into your email account.

What should you do?

### A

Only change the password

### B

Only delete the email app

### C

Change the password and sign out unfamiliar sessions

The best answer is:

## C
