# Your Email Is the Master Key

Your email account is often more important than it looks.

It may be connected to:

- Facebook
- shopping accounts
- banking alerts
- streaming services
- travel bookings
- cloud storage
- other online accounts

And most importantly...

## Password reset links often go to your email

---

## Think about this

You forget your Facebook password.

You click:

**Forgot password**

Where does the reset link usually go?

### Your email.

That means someone who controls your email may be able to reset passwords for other accounts too.

<div className="alert alert--warning" role="alert">

**If someone gets into your email, they may get a way into other accounts as well.**

</div>

---

## Your email can also reveal a lot

Inside your inbox there may be:

- receipts
- booking confirmations
- names of companies you use
- personal conversations
- account alerts
- password reset messages

This can help a criminal understand much more about you.

---

## So what should you do?

### Give your email account extra protection

A few simple steps make a big difference:

- Use a **unique password**
- Do not reuse that password anywhere else
- Turn on **two-step verification**
- Check that your recovery phone number is correct
- Check that your recovery email is still yours
- Review devices signed into the account

---

## Check recovery details before you need them

Recovery details are what help you get back into an account if something goes wrong.

For example:

- your mobile number
- another email address
- a trusted device

<div className="alert alert--info" role="alert">

People often only notice outdated recovery details **after** they lose access to an account.

It is much easier to check them while everything is working.

</div>

---

## Changing your phone?

Before replacing or resetting an old phone, check:

- Can you still access your email?
- Is your current phone number saved?
- Do you have another recovery option?
- Where are your passwords stored?
- Will your two-step verification move to the new phone?

This can prevent a lot of trouble later.

---

## What if someone gets into your email?

Start by securing the email account itself.

### Check:

1. Has the password been changed?
2. Has the recovery email changed?
3. Has the phone number changed?
4. Are there unfamiliar devices signed in?
5. Are there strange forwarding rules?
6. Has two-step verification been changed?

<div className="alert alert--warning" role="alert">

A criminal may try to keep access to an email account even after the password is changed.

That is why checking recovery details and signed-in devices matters too.

</div>
