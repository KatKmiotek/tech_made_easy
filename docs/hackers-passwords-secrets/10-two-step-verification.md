# Two-Step Verification

A password is one layer of protection.

Two-step verification adds another.

You may also see it called:

- 2FA
- two-factor authentication
- multi-factor authentication
- MFA

They all describe a similar idea:

## You need more than just the password

---

## What does "multi-factor" actually mean?

Security checks usually fall into a few different categories.

### Something you know

For example:

- a password
- a PIN
- a security answer

### Something you have

For example:

- your phone
- an authenticator app
- a security key
- a trusted device

### Something you are

For example:

- your fingerprint
- your face
- another biometric check

<div className="alert alert--info" role="alert">

**Multi-factor authentication is strongest when it uses different types of checks.**

For example:

**Something you know:** your password

plus

**Something you have:** your phone

</div>

---

## How does it work?

Imagine someone knows your password.

They try to log in.

The website then asks for something else, for example:

- a code sent to your phone
- an approval notification
- a code from an authenticator app
- Face ID
- fingerprint
- a security key

Without that second step, the password alone may not be enough.

<div className="alert alert--success" role="alert">

**This is why two-step verification is so useful.**

A stolen password does not automatically mean a stolen account.

</div>

---

## A simple example

You enter your email address and password.

Your password is:

**Something you know**

Then your phone shows:

> Is this you trying to sign in?

Your phone is:

**Something you have**

You choose:

**Yes**

or:

**No**

That extra check makes it much harder for someone else to get into the account.

---

## What types are there?

### Text message codes

You receive a code by SMS.

For example:

`483921`

You enter it on the website.

Your phone acts as the **something you have**.

---

### Authenticator apps

An app creates a temporary code which changes every few seconds.

Examples include:

- Microsoft Authenticator
- Google Authenticator
- Authy

---

### Approval notifications

Your phone asks you to approve or deny the login.

This is common with:

- Google
- Microsoft
- banking apps

---

### Face ID or fingerprint

Some services can use your phone or computer to confirm it is really you.

This is an example of:

**Something you are**

---

## Which one is best?

Any extra protection is usually much better than using only a password.

Authenticator apps and device-based approval are generally stronger than SMS codes.

But SMS two-step verification is still useful.

<div className="alert alert--info" role="alert">

If the choice is:

**SMS two-step verification**

or

**no two-step verification**

then SMS is usually the better choice.

</div>

---

## Very important: never share the code

A criminal may already know your password.

The only thing stopping them is the second step.

They might phone or message you and say:

> "I've just sent you a security code. Please read it back to me."

Do not give it to them.

<div className="alert alert--warning" role="alert">

**A verification code is for you.**

Banks, email providers and other companies should not need you to read your login code back to someone who contacted you unexpectedly.

</div>

---

## What if you receive a code you did not request?

Do not approve anything.

Do not give the code to anyone.

It may mean somebody is trying to log into your account.

That does not always mean they know your password, but it is worth paying attention.

---

## Check the message carefully

If your phone says:

> "Are you trying to sign in?"

and you are not...

choose:

**No**

or:

**Deny**

Then check your account security.

---

## What should you protect first?

If you only turn on two-step verification for a few accounts, start with:

1. Your main email account
2. Banking and financial accounts
3. Social media
4. Shopping accounts with saved payment details

---

## One common worry

### "What if I lose my phone?"

This is why recovery options matter.

Some services may offer:

- backup codes
- another trusted device
- a recovery email
- another phone number

Keep recovery information up to date.

<div className="alert alert--info" role="alert">

Two-step verification is very useful, but make sure you also know how you would recover your account if your phone was lost or replaced.

</div>

---

## Quick question

Which is safer?

### A

A strong password only

### B

A strong password plus another type of security check

The answer is:

## B

<div className="alert alert--success" role="alert">

**Easy way to remember MFA:**

**Something you know + something you have + something you are**

You will not always use all three, but using more than one type of check gives your account stronger protection.

</div>
